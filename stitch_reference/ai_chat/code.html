The **Krebs Cycle** (also known as the Citric Acid Cycle) is a key metabolic pathway that occurs in the **mitochondrial matrix**. It's the second stage of cellular respiration.

STEP 01: Acetyl-CoA (2C) joins with Oxaloacetate (4C) to form **Citrate (6C)**.
STEP 02: Citrate is oxidized, releasing **CO2** and forming **NADH**. Result: Alpha-ketoglutarate (5C).
STEP 03: Another CO2 is released, more **NADH** and **ATP** (or GTP) are produced. Result: Succinate (4C).
STEP 04: Oxidation continues, producing **FADH2** and a final **NADH** to regenerate Oxaloacetate.

For every turn of the cycle, you get **3 NADH, 1 FADH2, and 1 ATP**. Since one glucose creates two Acetyl-CoA, the cycle turns twice per glucose molecule.
